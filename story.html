The problem

One region. One document. Very different patients.

01 / 06

Two states

New York and New Jersey impose different PDMP duties — New York before every Schedule II–IV prescription, New Jersey at the first visit and quarterly.

02 / 06

Three drug classes

Opioids, benzodiazepines and ADHD stimulants each carry different risks — and some patients take all three.

03 / 06

A wide risk spectrum

Age, SUD history, concurrent controlled substances and PDMP findings vary enormously across a panel.

03

The risk tier

How the risk tier is suggested

Three questions, asked in order. The answer sets drug-screen frequency, pill counts, reassessment, specialist involvement and the review date.

01 / 04

First: any high-risk trigger?

  • Active SUD without specialist involvement
  • Opioid + benzodiazepine
  • Cross-class concurrent controlled substances
  • Prior lost-medication report
  • No indication at a dose ≥ threshold
02 / 04

Otherwise: any enhanced trigger?

  • Past or active SUD without specialist
  • Early-fill history
  • Dose ≥ threshold
  • Age ≥ 65
  • Inherited / presumptive indication
  • Same-class concurrent CS
03 / 04

Otherwise: Standard — and the prescriber decides

The tier is a suggestion with its reasons shown, never a verdict. The prescriber confirms or overrides it, and overrides are flagged.

04 / 04

Naloxone is suggested the same way

Offered automatically for any opioid + benzodiazepine combination, or for an opioid-involving class with a dose at or above threshold or any SUD history. The prescriber can override it either way.

SECTION 1 — SCOPE AND PARTIES

CONTROLLED SUBSTANCE PRESCRIBING AGREEMENT

This agreement is entered into between [PROVIDER NAME], [TITLE], of Crystal Run Healthcare, and [PATIENT NAME] (Date of Birth: [DOB]) on [DATE]. This agreement covers the following medication(s): [MEDICATION, DOSE, FREQUENCY].

This agreement is a clinical document establishing shared expectations for the prescribing relationship. It is advisory in nature; experienced providers retain clinical discretion. Nothing in this agreement limits the provider's ability to exercise independent clinical judgment.

Indication: This agreement covers [MEDICATION] prescribed for the management of [DIAGNOSED CONDITION], documented in the medical record.

SECTION 2 — PATIENT OBLIGATIONS

Single prescriber: I agree to obtain opioid prescriptions from a single prescriber — the provider named in this agreement. I will not seek prescriptions for opioid from any other provider without the knowledge and documented coordination of my prescribing provider.

Single pharmacy: I agree to fill all prescriptions for [MEDICATION] at a single pharmacy: [PHARMACY NAME AND LOCATION]. I will notify my provider in advance if I need to use a different pharmacy due to supply issues, travel, or insurance requirements, and will document the reason.

Use as prescribed: I agree to take [MEDICATION] only as prescribed — at the prescribed dose, frequency, and for the prescribed indication. I will not adjust my dose without discussing it with my provider first.

No sharing or transfer: I understand that sharing, selling, or otherwise transferring my controlled substance prescription to another person is a federal felony under 21 USC §841. I agree not to do so.

Safe storage: I agree to store [MEDICATION] in a secure location, out of reach of children and visitors. I understand that proper storage is my responsibility.

Disclosure: I agree to inform my provider of all other medications, supplements, and substances I am using, including over-the-counter medications, alcohol, cannabis, and any controlled substances prescribed by other providers.

Prescription monitoring: I understand that my provider is required by New York Public Health Law §3343-a (I-STOP) to review the New York State Prescription Monitoring Program registry before every prescription of this controlled substance. I consent to this review.

Drug screening: I agree to provide urine samples for drug screening when requested by my provider, at a frequency of approximately every 3 months or as clinically indicated. I understand that the purpose of this screening is to support safe prescribing, not to punish me.

Refill timing: I understand that my medication will be prescribed on a set schedule. I agree not to request early refills except in advance for documented travel or documented supply-chain issues. I understand that federal law (21 CFR §1306.12) permits multiple sequential prescriptions with earliest fill dates for up to 90 days of supply when clinically appropriate.

Lost or stolen medication: I understand that Crystal Run Healthcare's policy is not to replace lost or stolen controlled substance prescriptions. I understand that this is an institutional policy; federal law does not categorically prohibit replacement. If my medication is lost or stolen, I agree to report the loss to my provider and, if stolen, to file a police report.

Pill counts: I agree to bring my medication to any appointment when requested for a pill count. I understand that pill counts may be requested without advance notice.

Treatment goals: I understand that the goal of this medication is to support my daily function — my ability to work, maintain relationships, and carry out daily activities — and to manage [CONDITION]. I understand that improvement in function, even if pain or symptoms are not fully eliminated, is a meaningful treatment outcome. I agree to discuss my functional status at each visit.

SECTION 3 — PROVIDER OBLIGATIONS

PDMP review: My provider will review the New York State Prescription Monitoring Program registry before every prescription of this controlled substance, as required by NY Public Health Law §3343-a.

Functional assessment: My provider will assess my functional status — including my ability to work, maintain relationships, carry out daily activities, and drive safely — at each visit.

Reassessment: My provider will reassess the benefit and risk of continuing this medication at least every 6 months, or sooner if concerns emerge.

SECTION 4 — MONITORING AND GRADUATED RESPONSE

Monitoring purpose: The monitoring activities described in this agreement — PDMP review, urine drug screening, pill counts, functional assessment — are safety tools, not punitive measures. Unexpected monitoring findings will be discussed with me directly before any clinical decisions are made. My provider will apply clinical judgment to each finding and will not dismiss me from care on the basis of a single monitoring result.

Response to concerns: If a monitoring finding, pattern of requests, or clinical concern arises, my provider will follow a graduated approach:

(1) First concern: Direct conversation, documentation, and reassessment of the monitoring plan. No automatic change to the prescription.

(2) Repeated or escalating concern: Formal review within the controlled substance agreement framework, urine drug screen if not already done, PDMP review, and discussion of whether the current management plan remains appropriate. Specialist input may be sought.

(3) Significant or unresolved concern: Specialist consultation before continuing. Continuation may not be appropriate without specialist co-management. If I am not able to continue in this practice, my provider will ensure I receive a referral or bridge plan — I will not be abruptly abandoned.

Age-related risks: My provider has discussed with me that benzodiazepines and sedating medications carry increased risks of falls, fractures, and cognitive effects in adults over 65, as noted in the American Geriatrics Society Beers Criteria. I understand these risks and have discussed them with my provider. My provider and I have agreed on the clinical rationale for continuing this medication at this time, and will reassess annually or sooner if my functional status changes.

SECTION 5 — ACKNOWLEDGMENT AND SIGNATURES

I have read this agreement and have had the opportunity to ask questions. I understand its contents and agree to its terms. I understand that this agreement may be updated at any time with my participation, and that I will be given a copy of any updated agreement to review before signing.

I understand that this agreement is a clinical document, not a legal contract, and that my provider retains clinical discretion at all times.

Graduated response to concerns

S4-FIXED-GRADUATED-RESPONSE

Response to concerns: If a monitoring finding, pattern of requests, or clinical concern arises, my provider will follow a graduated approach:

(1) First concern: Direct conversation, documentation, and reassessment of the monitoring plan. No automatic change to the prescription.

(2) Repeated or escalating concern: Formal review within the controlled substance agreement framework, urine drug screen if not already done, PDMP review, and discussion of whether the current management plan remains appropriate. Specialist input may be sought.

Governance

Safeguards built into the design

Clinician stays in charge

Tier and naloxone are suggestions with visible rationale. Overrides are allowed and flagged. The header and acknowledgment state the agreement is advisory and preserves clinical discretion.

Non-punitive by design

Every agreement includes the monitoring-framing and graduated-response blocks: no dismissal on a single result, conversation before decisions, and a referral or bridge plan rather than abandonment.

Honest about authority

19 of 36 blocks are flagged proposed institutional standard — they rest on convention or institutional policy rather than a national mandate. These are the blocks that need this committee's endorsement.

Versioned & Epic-ready

Signatures carry the library version (v1.0.0 (2026-05-25)). Each configuration maps to a SmartPhrase name such as .CSAGREE-OPT-NY-STD; bracketed fields are completed at signing.
